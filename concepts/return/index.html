Lesson 3: Return

The return is every reward still to come, added up. The discount makes rewards further away count less.

In this game: From the start there are three routes: a coin 2 steps away, a chest 6 steps away, and a longer way round to the same chest, 8 steps. Every step costs 1.

  1. In step 1, send the robot to the coin and to the chest and read each total. The room plays those two routes; the long way is worked out in the picture below.
  2. In step 2, watch the chest and the coin shrink in the room.
  3. In step 3, drag the discount down until the robot picks the coin.

What you saw

1. Every route has its own return

Coin

Chest
−1−1−1−1−1+10= 5

Same start, three routes. The robot takes the one with the highest return: the short way to the chest. The long way goes three right, three down, one right and one up, 8 steps in all, so it pays two more steps than the short way.

2. The return looks forward

Two steps along the chest route
−1−1−1−1−1+10= 7

The two steps already paid are struck out. They happened, so they do not count. Only what is still to come does: −1 −1 −1 + 10 = 7.

3. Further away counts less

With a discount of 0.9, a reward keeps 90% of its worth for every step you wait. This is what 10 points count as, by how far away they are.

Each column counts the same three routes again with that discount. The marked number is the route the robot takes. The lower the discount, the more the far chest shrinks, and below about 0.85 the near coin wins.

4. Why discount at all

  • The future is less sure. A reward 20 steps away may never arrive: the robot can slip, or the game can end first.
  • Some tasks never end. Add up rewards forever and every route totals infinity. A discount keeps the total a number you can compare.
  • It sets patience. At 0.5 the agent cares about the next few steps. At 0.99 it plans far ahead.
  • Compare routes. Each has its own return. Take the highest.
  • Look forward. Count only the rewards still to come.
  • Discount. The further away a reward is, the less it counts.

In code: the same idea in Python

 
def ret(rewards, gamma):
    return sum(r * gamma**t for t, r in enumerate(rewards))

routes = {
    "coin":        [-1, 2],
    "chest":       [-1] * 5 + [10],
    "chest, long": [-1] * 7 + [10],
}

for gamma in (1, 0.9, 0.5):
    returns = {name: round(ret(r, gamma), 2) for name, r in routes.items()}
    print(gamma, returns, "best:", max(returns, key=returns.get))

The same three routes. At 1 and 0.9 the chest wins; at 0.5 the coin does.

The lesson: the return adds up what is still to come. The discount decides how far ahead the robot cares, and that can change what it chooses.
