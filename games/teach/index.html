Step 1: drive one lap without crashing. The camera follows the car, so Left always turns left. Every moment you drive becomes one example for the brain to learn from. Arrow keys and A/D work too.

Examples
0
Moments the brain can learn from. Each one pairs what the car sensed (six numbers) with how you steered at that moment. Your lap gives about 850; every time you take the wheel later adds more.
Loss
–
How far the brain's steering is from yours, averaged over every example (half the squared gap). Zero would mean it copies you exactly. Training is the job of pushing this number down.
Distance
0 of 2 laps
How far the car has got on this drive: out of one lap while you teach, and out of two laps when the brain drives on its own.

Training

Learning rate

How big a step each weight takes downhill on every update. Too small and the loss crawls down; too big and the steps overshoot, so the loss jumps around or gets worse. Change it after it has learned and it learns your same lap again from a fresh brain, so you can compare.

0.05: steady

Its brain

Six numbers in (five feelers and speed), 8 hidden neurons, one number out: steering. 65 weights in all. Each line is a weight: solid pushes up, dashed pushes down, brighter means more signal right now. While you drive, it is only guessing; watch the lines change while it learns.

What it would steer right now, next to what you steer.

Loss

One point per epoch: one full pass over all your examples. A line falling to a low, flat floor means it has learned what your examples can teach. It never reaches zero, because you never steer exactly the same way twice in the same spot.
