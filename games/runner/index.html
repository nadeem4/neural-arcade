Coin Runner

A hero learns a platformer level from points alone, and finds loopholes when the points are wrong. Teaches reward, exploration, policy gradients and reward hacking.

The setup
A hero that has never played. It senses 12 simple things and can wait, run, leap (run and jump) or jump on the spot.
The rule
Every try earns points from your rules. Moves from tries that scored better than usual become more likely.

Rules

Three ready-made sets of points. Balanced pays mostly for the flag, so it learns to finish. Coin hungry pays far more for coins than for the flag, and the block above the start pays a coin every bump: watch it farm. Afraid of dying makes dying very costly and pays for every second alive: watch it never move. When it does something you didn't mean, because your rules paid for it, that is called reward hacking. Changing the rules starts a fresh brain.

Pays mostly for reaching the flag.

Practice speed

Learning takes about a thousand tries. While you watch one try at real speed, more tries run unseen in the background, and it learns from all of them. The try on screen always plays with what the brain knows so far. "Only what you see" is slow, but you can follow every try.

About 30 unseen tries a second

Its brain

Twelve numbers in (what it senses), 8 hidden neurons, four out: one per move. 140 weights in all. The four outputs become chances, and it picks a move by rolling the dice, about eight times a second. That randomness is how it explores. Learning shifts the chances towards moves that led to more points.

How tries end

One bar for every 50 tries, split by how they ended: reached the flag, died, or ran out of time (20 seconds). Watch the mix change as it learns, and change again when you change the rules.
