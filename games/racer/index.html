Neural Racer

Ten cars with random brains learn to lap a track by breeding. Teaches fitness, selection, mutation and overfitting.

The setup
Ten cars get random brains. Nobody teaches them how to drive.
The rule
After each round, only the cars that drove furthest have children.

Population

How many cars try in each round. More cars means more random tries, so a good driver usually turns up sooner, but each round takes more work to run.

Few enough to follow every car. Usually learns within a handful of rounds.

Mutation

After a child gets its weights from its two parents, this share of them is nudged by a small random amount. A little mutation brings new ideas. None means nothing new can ever appear. Too much scrambles what the parents passed on.

Its brain

This brain is driving the orange car. Left: the six numbers it senses, its five feelers on the track plus its speed. Middle: 8 hidden neurons that mix them. Right: the two things it controls, steering and throttle. Each line is a weight: solid pushes the next neuron up, dashed pushes it down, brighter means more signal right now. The weights never change while it drives; only breeding changes them.
